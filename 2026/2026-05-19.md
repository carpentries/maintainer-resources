* Open discussion:
    * What is (most) helpful in a transition?
        * Historical and organizational knowledge. Relying on the previous people to share knowledge.
        * "Be the change you want to see in the world" > braveness to transition  / make the first step
        * Documentation on the process of a transition (what worked, what went wrong, error logging)
        * A checklist before with what you think will happen (although there might happen thing you don't know yet)
        * Could reach out to original developers of the lesson
        * Proactively engage with people in your environment. Socialization is important!
        * Be prepared for asynchronous and sporadic work
        * Onboarding and meeting with experienced co-maintainers
        * Keep a log of what happened during the transition
   * Continuing Maintainers, what tips you want to share with new Maintainers?
       * Come to the monthly meetings to learn a lot, e.g., GitHub tricks and polite responses to pull requests
       * Meet synchronously with co-maintainers to get to know each other and discuss the state of the lesson, what needs to be done, what issues we could tackle, what changes we want to make, what you want to bring to it
       * Don't know if this is the best advice, but the Library Carpentry Governance Committee helped me to get back on track with the lesson
           * get an overview what needs to be done and find help
           * but this was only for getting the lesson to a specfic state (beta), so probably not the address to go to with everything
       * Do some lesson sprints from time to time where you prepare some issues that you work on together in 1-2 hours and focus on hands-on work 
       * Sometimes it also helps to show the lesson to someone who is not familiar with it at all to get a fresh look on it
       * Keep in mind that this has been taught many times, so there is not a need to be in a rush to change things
           * This doesn't mean you can't change anything!
           * But it's good to keep in mind the lesson has been going well and is fine
  * New Maintainers, what questions do you have for continuing Maintainers?
    * How has genAI changed maintenance?
        * Working on a policy for generated PRs
            * We will accept such submissions as long as they are labeled as such
            * The contributor must have done the work of verifying and reviewing the generated contribution (extractative contributions) - the burden of reviewing and testing shouldn't be solely on the maintainers
    * Do you find any use for AI tools for maintenance?
        * I definitely used it to save some time for instructions e.g. instruction on a wikidata tool and it worked pretty well, but you need to check the instructions yourself to see that all links work and there is no halluzination in it
        * Grouping issues to make clusters of issues to help me determine what issues could be tackled at the same time or were similar
