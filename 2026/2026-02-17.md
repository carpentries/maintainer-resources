#### 3. General Discussion
 
- Toby: AI contributions policy for Carpentries repositories
    * related reading: [_An AI Agent Published a Hit Piece on Me_](https://theshamblog.com/an-ai-agent-published-a-hit-piece-on-me/)
    * With growing prevalence of AI-generated contributions to Open Source projects, I want to develop and publish a policy on whether and when to accept contributions to Carpentries projects that have been generated by or with the assistance of genAI. I need input from Maintainers to help formulate a policy that will work and that you are happy with.
    * Have you received contributions to your lesson (or another project that you maintain) that were generated -- or that you suspect were generated -- by AI?
        * Yes:
        * No: +++
        * Daniel: not in the lesson repository, but have received AI-generated PRs to repositories that I work on as part of my job. matplotlib example above is relevant to The Carpentries too, because it is about learning. Main reason the PR was closed was because it addressed a 'good-first-issue' intended for newcomers to the project.
    * The matplotlib project has a policy that all contributions must have come from a human to be accepted, to ensure that somebody can be held responsible for the contribution. [Full policy from matplotlib](https://matplotlib.org/devdocs/devel/contribute.html#restrictions-on-generative-ai-usage). 
      Would this policy work for The Carpentries? 
      In contrast to matplotlib/scientific Python projects, most of our repository content is prose-like (e.g. lesson material): does this mean our policy should be substantially different from theirs?
          * Alex: AI can be useful for translating lesson content into another (natural) language. Would need a human with sufficient knowledge of the language to review and approve translations.
              * Note from Toby: this is pretty much the workflow we have on CrowdIn for the ongoing lesson internationalisation projects.
          * Jose: it will not always be easy to identify use of AI with absolute confidence. We should not be too restrictive. A policy that stes out the expectation that a person will need to be responsible for the contribution.
          * Alex: I would be concerned about any policy that placed burden on Maintainers to determine use of AI.
          * Daniel: This is a big question. Depends on the nature of the contribution. I think maintainability aspect applies regardless of the type of content: it gives us confidence if we can call on the person who made a change to return to the conversation in future if that content is discussed again. Other implication is the licensing issue: was the model trained on licensed content without permission of the authors.
		* Annajiat: Without proper dection and coverage tools, its challenging. Turnitin started ignoring <=20% AI Aas that has higher chances of false positive. For the legal part, need to seprate similarity and AI generation. Need proper tooling.

	* What would you consider to be acceptable uses of genAI in contributions to your lesson repository?
	    * Jose: risk of plagiarism makes me inclined to be cautious. I would have accepted any contribution that a person is willing to be responsible for and that has been reviewed, but I would now say that is too broad. 
	    * Daniel: Rewording/rephrasing of technical content for a non-expert audience.
		* Annajiat: cited, explained, perhaps with the prompt as greg suggested i think. Special note about AI part whether it was used entirely or for style.
		* Alex: Phrasing, especially for people making contributions not in their first language.

* Final note: any policy restricting genAI use in contributions may not be entirely enforceable, but I think it is worth having one anyway. A policy will empower Maintainers to act on issues/PRs where they suspect contributions go against it, and inclusion of the policy in our CONTRIBUTING.md will provide guidance to contributors on how they should proceed in our projects.

Feel free to suggest topics you’re interested in discussing with other maintainers.
